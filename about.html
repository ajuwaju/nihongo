how to use the room

  1. 01
    kanji opens on the grid for your level. tap any character for its readings, words and a space for your own mnemonic. flashcards shows what is due plus today's new ones; grade honestly, the spacing does the rest. writing pad gives you the meaning and asks for the shape. quick quiz is twelve questions.
  2. 02
    vocab works the same way. flashcards run japanese → english by default; switch to english → japanese once a word feels easy.
  3. 03
    grammar lists every pattern with its meaning, how it attaches, examples you can hear, and a short drill. tick a point when it sits right. drill the blanks mixes fifteen questions from the whole level.
  4. 04
    articles are for reading with help: furigana on, dotted words show their meaning on hover, english one tap away. mark as read, answer the questions.
  5. 05
    reading is the exam version: a clock, no furigana, questions, and only afterwards the words and the english.
  6. 06
    listening uses the japanese voice on your own computer. play twice with the script hidden, answer, then reveal and read along. dictation trains one line at a time.
  7. 07
    quizzes build a mix of anything, or sit the mock exam: forty questions in jlpt order with thirty minutes on the clock.
  8. 08
    notes is the drawer. every kanji, grammar point, article and track has an "add a note" button that files a card here already linked back to the thing.

keyboard: in flashcards space flips and 1 to 4 grade. in quizzes a to d or 1 to 4 pick and enter moves on. esc closes a reader.

the fast route, week by week

the levels are roughly these sizes. n5 and n4 together are the foundation; n3 is where reading becomes real; n2 is the one employers ask for; n1 is mostly breadth.

  1. n5
    ~100 kanji · ~700 words · ~85 patterns. 2 to 3 weeks at 15 new cards a day. goal: read a short diary entry out loud and understand it.
  2. n4
    ~180 kanji · ~700 words · ~110 patterns. 4 weeks. goal: て-form, potential, conditionals and giving/receiving come without thinking.
  3. n3
    ~370 kanji · ~700 words here (real n3 is wider) · ~130 patterns. 8 weeks. goal: a news-style article without english.
  1. n2
    ~370 kanji · ~600 words here · ~150 patterns. 10 weeks. goal: essays and formal letters, keigo in and out.
  2. n1
    ~1000 kanji · ~600 words here · ~150 patterns. 16 weeks or more, mostly reading volume. goal: editorials, literary registers, written-only patterns.

a day that works (40 to 60 minutes)

  1. 10
    kanji flashcards until the due pile is empty, then today's new ones.
  2. 10
    vocab flashcards, same.
  3. 10
    one new grammar point: read it, hear the examples, do the drill, tick it. every third day do drill the blanks instead.
  4. 10
    one article (read aloud, questions) or one reading drill on exam days.
  5. 10
    one listening track, script hidden first. dictate one line.
  6. ∞
    sunday: mock exam at your target level. 80% twice in a row means move the level tab up and keep the old reviews running.

on going fast

speed comes from never skipping the review pile, not from bigger new-card numbers. if reviews take more than fifteen minutes, lower new cards per day for a week. read everything out loud; it fixes words three times faster than silent reading. and write kanji on the pad at least once each, your hand remembers what your eyes forget.

the kana

tap any kana to hear it. learn these first, hiragana then katakana. a week each at most. dakuten (゛) voices a row: か→が, さ→ざ, た→だ, は→ば, and handakuten (゜) makes は→ぱ. small ゃゅょ after an い-sound blend: き+ゃ=きゃ.

how this site treats you

the study files are encrypted with the room password and only decrypted inside your browser. nothing is loaded from any other website: no fonts, no scripts, no analytics, no embeds. the voice that reads japanese is the one installed on your own computer.

there are no cookies on the static site. the only things stored are the unlocked key (for this tab, or on this device if you tick "keep this device unlocked") and your profile's progress, in your own browser's storage.

what is in here

the jlpt stopped publishing official lists in 2010, so every "n5 kanji list" is an estimate. the level splits here follow the lists most textbooks and study sites share. readings use the kanjidic conventions: katakana for on-readings, hiragana for kun, a dot before okurigana (た.べる).

all passages, dialogues and drills were written for this site. if you spot a mistake, make a note card with the tag fix and it can be corrected in the content folder.
